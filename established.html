1. Does the estimator reach the exact answer, and with which sampler?

Where an exact likelihood exists, MCEM on the surrogate proposal reaches its maximum. Under cr and dd at 20-100 tips, 27 of 30 cell x proposal rows meet C1; the surrogate gives up a median 0.00-0.07 nats at 20-50 tips and 0.05-0.32 nats at 100, with rates within 0-7.5% (speciation) and 0-10.5% (extinction) of the exact estimate (E1). From 25 to 800 tips under cr the MCEM estimate sits within a tenth of the exact MLE's own spread, offset upward by 1-6% of the speciation rate, although the cells from 100 tips are clock-limited (S). The reported log-likelihood is the exact one to 10\(^{-12}\) under cr (E1), and on E40's real complete trees to 0.000 (cr) and within 0.01 (dd); where \(\rho\) < 1 the reported value sits 0.01-0.17 nats above DDD's because the two codes model sampling differently (E42).

The surrogate is the proposal that keeps its draws. It keeps 200 of 200 effective draws under cr at every size and sampling fraction, while thinning falls below 10 by 100-200 tips (E4, E6); over 67 cells of size, turnover and ED effect it falls below 10 in 7, all with a strong negative ED effect, against 54 for thinning (E7). It is ranked behind thinning only for ed below \(\rho\) ≈ 0.8 (E6). Per E-step it costs 4.7-7.0 times thinning under cr, but per effective draw it is the cheaper from 100 tips (E4). For D models the surrogate gives the larger sample but failed its adoption rule, so thinning stays their default (E19); K models can only use thinning. Between the two thinning variants for ned, the ED-aware one is ahead on 77% of trees, not the 95% the rule required (E3).

Convergence is slow in iterations and noisy per iterate. Per-iterate noise falls about as 1/√draws (1-7% across rates; extinction 2-7 times speciation), and no settling criterion gives a stable scaling (E13). The mc_error rule never stopped while the trace was still moving (0 of 17) and lands about 2% from a 400-iteration reference (E9). The time of a lineage-level fit is in its starting point: one ned iteration from the box takes 77 s, 78 min and 5.1 h at 100, 200 and 400 tips while its parts take under two minutes (E15); the ladder's nested starts follow from this. Most lineage-level fits in the study did not meet the stopping rule within their budget (see every "clock-limited" and "unfinished" marking), and one nd fit ran 60 hours without its clock firing (E30).

Two shipped defaults fail their own rules. A fixed relaxation step of ⅛ converges 107 of 139 diverging mean fields at 1000 sweeps against 77 for "auto", so the pre-set rule changes the default, yet the package still ships "auto" (E10); a mean field that converges only under relaxation leaves an effective sample of about 1 either way. The ED-aware thinning ordering fails rule C4 (E3).

2. Are lineage-level coefficients recovered and selected?

The ED coefficient is estimable; selecting ned over dd needs a strong effect. At turnover 0.10 the 0.80 selection contour lies between -0.35\(\lambda\) and -0.60\(\lambda\) at 100, 200 and 400 tips, with false positives at 3.3% (E12); at turnover 0.25 and 100 tips it lies between -0.35\(\lambda\) (0.67) and -0.60\(\lambda\) (1.00) (E16). At -0.15\(\lambda\) selection is 0.05-0.55 (E12) and centring ED does not lift it (0.10-0.20; E18). The coefficient's sign is recovered (≥ 0.95 at |effect| ≥ 0.15\(\lambda\)) while its size is attenuated on unfinished fits (0.69, 0.40, 0.19 at -0.60\(\lambda\) from 100 to 400 tips) and comes back with more iterations (0.96, 0.67, 0.72 at -0.35\(\lambda\) in E12b) (E12); on the 7 fits that finished at 200 tips it is 1.02 (E29). All these rates are floors: every E12, E16 and E18 cell is clock-limited.

Specificity holds. On another group's null trees (evesim) ned is selected on 0.05 and 0.00 of trees (E11), and the two simulators agree in distribution (E14). False positives for ned are 2 of 60 null trees in E12 (2 of 20 at 100 tips) and 0 of 15 in E16; for nedc, edc and ed (exact simulator) 0-1 of 20 (E18, E24). The exception is d, at 0.15 (E24).

Of the single-covariate models, edc meets every half of its rule (ratio 0.96 and 1.02, selection 0.95 and 0.85, no false positive); ed under the exact simulator recovers (0.85, 1.10) and is selected at -0.35 (1.00) but not at +0.35 (0.75); d recovers at +0.35 only; k meets every half at -0.35 and is sampler-limited at +0.35 (E24). The first ed results came from a simulator that held rates fixed between events and produced clades 30% too large; E24b replaces them (E24).

Turnover and incomplete sampling are where recovery breaks. At turnover 0.5 the thinning-based ned fits of E2 are biased by 1-4 SD and null siblings select ned on up to 0.9 of trees; at \(\rho\) 0.7 the effective sample falls below 10 outside the -0.15\(\lambda\) cells. In the ladder study the ned and nk cells at \(\rho\) 0.75 and 0.50 are sampler-limited (E27). Fitted extinction slopes, generated at zero, come back away from zero for ed (-0.25 to -0.73 per unit), d (+0.36) and nk (-0.06 to -0.15 per split) (E24, E21).

3. The pendant-age model and the ancestral-splits covariate

nd: the sign of \(\beta\)_D is recovered, its size is not, and no fit has finished. At ±0.35 the ratio is 0.30 and 0.47 on thinning and 0.43 and 0.63 on the surrogate with a 30-minute clock (E19); with 300 iterations and a four-hour clock it is 0.67 (-0.35) and 0.80 (+0.35, sampler-limited), with 0 of 39 fits meeting the stopping rule (E30). On the first 4 of 40 fits at 1000 iterations none finished and \(\beta\)_D moved by -0.04 to +0.03 on the three with 39-106 effective draws (E37, stopped after the D fix changed the likelihood it maximises). At 200 tips the ladder chooses nd on 5 of 20 of its own trees and the pendant-edge pair nd + ned on 9, with \(\beta\)_D at 0.68 whether the fits ran to a quarter of their iterations or all 300 (E31). In the pairwise contest nd beats dd on 19 of 19 trees at -0.35 (E30), while in the ladder at 100 tips it is chosen on 0 of 10 (dd on 7) (E23).

D and ED share the pendant edge. On the path, the mixed generator returns D alone on 0.80 and both on 0.15, so D and ED are not separable at 100 tips (E20); at 400 tips D replaces ED as the first covariate on ned trees (E22). Within a tree their rank correlation is 0.57-0.74 (E39).

K: recovered, selected on most of its own trees, short of 0.80. By fitting, \(\beta\)_K comes back at 0.95 and 0.92 of its value at ∓0.35 with selection over dd on 0.15 and 0.60 (E21, E21a, clock-limited). In the ladder nk is chosen on 7 of 10 trees at 100 tips (E23) and on 11-15 of 20 in the six readable conditions of E27, where \(\beta\)_K returns at 0.65-0.76 at 50-200 tips, 1.04 at turnover 0.25 and 1.30 at 0.40. The two pages differ on the ratio (0.92-0.95 in E21a, 0.65-0.76 in E27 at the reference cell and beside it); the designs differ in box, clock and in whether the fit is the ladder's. On the path K is admitted on at most 1 of 20 trees of other generators, and on nk trees ED enters first and on every tree, carrying the K signal with the opposite sign (E21, E21b).

4. Model choice: the ladder and the path

Monte Carlo bias does not decide AIC rankings. Correcting the Jensen gaps moves rankings by 0.01-0.25 AIC; it would reverse 1 of 101 wrong choices, a tie at 0.003 AIC, and the guard flagged 0 of 240 rankings. The guard does not warn on variance: leaders on fewer than 10 effective draws pass silently (E8).

The reading matters. At 200 tips the minimum-AIC reading admits nd on 3 of 12 null trees, the parsimonious reading (fewest parameters within 2 AIC) on 0 (E23); across E27's cells the parsimonious reading keeps lineage-level false positives at 0-2 of 20 while the minimum fails at the reference and 50-tip cells (7 and 6 of 20) (E27). compare_models() reports the parsimonious choice from emphasis f30a791.

The ladder recovers the clade-level answer and nk, not ned or nd. At 100 tips the generator is chosen on 0.80 of cr and 0.90 of dd trees (E23). Null trees keep dd on at least 16 of 20 at the reference, family-like, 50%-sampling and 200-tip cells, and move to cr at turnover 0.25 and 0.40 (8 and 6 of 20 keep dd) (E27). ned is chosen on at most 2 of 20 of its own trees in any condition although its coefficient is recovered at 0.91-0.98 in three cells (E27). A ladder costs a median 47 minutes at 100 tips and 3.4 hours at 200 (E23).

The dgLARS path selects the null and N, and not the lineage-level covariates. At the generating parameters the null is chosen on 0.85-1.00 of cr trees from 50 to 400 tips; on dd trees N is in the set on 0.85-1.00 but N alone on only 0.40-0.70; ned's N + ED is never chosen on 0.80 (E20, E22). Taken at a fitted estimate, as a user would, dd's exact set falls to 0.35 and 0.25 and absent covariates enter on 0.45-0.55, so the path is not usable as a route by its rule (E33). On real trees the path and the ladder name different covariates: Corvidae's ladder chooses nk while its path keeps D and ED (E25); on E40's 170 trees the path's BIC set equals the ladder's choice on 16 (Real phylogenies).

5. The extinction side, conditioning and the information a tree carries

At 200 tips and turnover 0.10 extinction is not identified, by any estimator. The fitted extinction rate is a median 26 times below its generating 0.05, with the ED slope on its box limit, on finished fits as on the others (E29); widening the box moves the estimates with it for a median log-likelihood gain of 0.01, and an extinction slope on ED is returned on the box limit whatever its generating value (E32). The exact bd_ML puts extinction at zero on 37 of 40 of the same trees; dd_ML puts it 2.4-3.2 times too high (E32). Across E27's cells, 188 of 300 lineage-level fits have an extinction parameter on a limit (E29).

Neither conditioning nor the emulator is the cause. Conditioning on survival brings the ned fit's extinction rate within a factor of 2 of 0.05 on 1 of 20 trees (0 of 20 under the corrected emulator) (E34). The package's survival emulator labelled clades that pass the lineage cap as extinct; with them labelled survived and 2000 training clades it meets its accuracy rule in all four exact cells, and emphasis d3dee92 ships that (E36, E36c). Through any emulator, the exact survival function included, the conditioned cr estimate spreads from the box floor to above 0.1 on these trees (E36).

The information map is of the trees. With the exact constant-rate likelihood, extinction is detected (zero excluded on ≥ 80% of trees) never up to 1600 tips at turnover 0.10, from 1600 at 0.25, from 400 at 0.40 and from 100 at 0.50-0.60; it is bounded within a factor of 2 only from 400- 1600 tips at turnover ≥ 0.40. Under exact diversity dependence it is detected on every tree from 100 tips at every turnover (E38). The study's 200-tip, turnover-0.10 trees sit where cr detection is 5%; on E40's 16 smallest real trees 0 of 16 exclude zero extinction (E41). The size study reads the cr extinction rate as recovered from 100 tips at turnover 0.40 where E38 detects it on 40% of trees; the two use different criteria (a median over trees against a per-tree test) (S, E38).

Where trees hold more extinction, a K slope appears in one direction. At turnover 0.25 the negative K slope returns at 0.75 of its value with the right sign on 18 of 20 and the zero slope at +0.004, while the positive arm is sampler-limited, so by the rule the slope is not identified (E35); at turnover 0.10 the negative slope returned at 0.36 (E32). The supplement calls the turnover-0.25 result "the first extinction slope recovered", which is stronger than the rule allows. The extinction path does not detect an ED driver; it admits D instead on 0.50-0.70 of trees where extinction rises with ED (E33).

A sampling fraction off by 0.1 does not change the chosen model (same choice on ≥ 18 of 20 trees), and changes \(\beta\)_N by a median 19% when told 0.82 instead of 0.92 (5 of 20 trees outside the rule's 0.75-1.25 band) and \(\beta\)_K by 5-8%; the fitted extinction rate does not register the change (E35, part b).

On complete trees, and the exact estimate on real ones (2026-10-15)

With nothing hidden, every model's likelihood returns its generating coefficients once the D likelihood holds D constant between events (E44; before the change d returned \(\beta_D\) at 0.70 and 1.46 of its value, a candidate explanation for the \(\beta_D\) shortfall of every D experiment). Raw ED shows a per-tree ridge between \(\beta_0\) and \(\beta_{ED}\) that centring removes. On E40's real trees, MCEM reaches the exact maximum for cr and dd (E42), and the exact dd maximum puts extinction at zero as the fits do.

6. Real trees

The workflow runs on deposited family trees. On 46 of 47 Condamine family trees the cr fit is within 0.53 nats of bd_ML (E5). On the linear link ned beat dd on Corvidae and Icteridae by 18.6 and 14.7 AIC, margins inside the central 90% of each clade's own bootstrap (E17); these fits are superseded by the exponential-link ladder, where Corvidae goes to nk (by 12.0 over ned), Icteridae to ned and Cotingidae to dd (E25, E28).

Across families, a lineage-level model is chosen in about half the usable trees, and never a pendant-age model by minimum AIC. On the merged table 21 of 37 usable families choose a lineage-level model, K in 12 and ED in 9; K raises speciation in 30 of 31 readable nk fits and ED lowers it in 30 of 32 ned fits (E26). For the 29 families inside the calibrated range, refitted with 300 iterations, 25 are usable and the parsimonious choice is cr 4, dd 5, nd 1, ned 8, nk 7; the minimum-AIC split of the 18 lineage-level verdicts is ned 8, nk 10; the choice is the same as E26's on 19 of 21 comparable families (E28). Which split a paper quotes is not decided (Open issues). Only 24 of 141 lineage-level fits met the stopping rule, and E27 measured K at 0.65-0.76 of its value on such fits, so the coefficients are read as attenuated (E26, E28). Extinction is not read: fitted turnover is below 0.05 in 41 of 47 families (E26).

On real trees K and ED overlap. Their within-tree rank correlation is a median -0.44 on the family trees against -0.18 on reconstructed simulated trees, and stronger the less balanced the tree (-0.51 across families), so a K choice and an ED choice on an unbalanced family tree are not distinct mechanisms (E39). E40's coefficients track imbalance in the same direction: over its 170 trees the K factor rises with it (rank correlation 0.53 over 124 read nk fits) and the ED factor falls (-0.57 over 145 read ned fits) (Real phylogenies).

What is still open

Running or unread experiments (status at 2026-10-19, 10:05):

  • E40: closed at 170 of 175 (five hung fits stopped by hand); the pairs rule fails (same choice on 10 of 29 pairs, rule 70%), so the class-tree clades are reported and not read; see Real phylogenies.
  • E41: closed at the 16-tree pilot (neither rule triggers; the box stays); the 154 trees beyond it are not run (decided 2026-10-19).
  • E43: emulators trained; the Monte Carlo reference in its third round of chunks.
  • E45: 643 of 1720 fits (about 60 a day); the cr cells of 200 tips and more and every dd cell are still running; the dd cells move to E43's host once its reference finishes (decided 2026-10-19).
  • E36 (b) was not rerun after the emulator fix d3dee92.

Questions the pages name and no experiment answers:

  • whether D and ED separate at any size or effect (E20, E22, E31);
  • whether the simulation results hold on trees as unbalanced as the family trees (E39);
  • whether a fitted ned reproduces its real margin against an alternative generator (a K or pendant-age model) (E17);
  • the size boundary (200 tips) and sampling boundary (\(\rho\) 0.4) for ned on the thinning proposal (E2, paper tier not run);
  • the draw-schedule arm of E15 (not run);
  • whether mean-field relaxation should be reported to the caller (E10);
  • every recovery, selection and extinction experiment uses crown age 15, while 43 of the 47 families are older (E17 is the only run at real crown ages);
  • whether the D experiments run before the D fix (E19, E24's d arm, E30, E31, D on the path, E40's nd fits) change under it: E44 shows the old likelihood biased \(\beta_D\) on complete trees; the estimability study measures d and nd afresh.
