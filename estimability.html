The question. For each model separately — cr, dd, d, nd, ed, edc, ned, nedc, k, nk — how well are its parameters estimated, and from how many tips is each estimate reliable, when the likelihood is the one conditioned on the survival of the clade.

This page is the plan and its status board. Each step becomes an experiment page when it runs; the board below links them and is updated as results come in.

Why the existing experiments do not answer it

  • Unconditioned fits. Every estimation experiment maximised \(f(y \mid \theta)\); only E34 and E36 conditioned, and only to measure the difference.
  • The conditioning factor is validated only for cr and dd. E36c measured the emulator against the exact survival probability: 90th-percentile error in \(\log P\) of 0.26-0.28 at 2000 training clades after the label fix. For the lineage-level models there is no exact value, and against 400-clade Monte Carlo references no learner met the adequacy rule (E36 a). E43 measured the package's emulator for them against a reference with a standard error of 0.01 and across 20 trainings: no training size meets the rule.
  • Simulations conditioned on more than survival. Several designs kept a tree only if its size fell in a window (E27, E38); no likelihood carries that condition.
  • D was defined two ways (until emphasis 4e0b9dd). The simulator held a lineage's centred pendant age \(D\) constant between events, as its definition implies (\(D_s = E_s - M\), and both grow at unit rate); the likelihood held \(M\) at its end-of-segment value, so \(D\) grew within a segment. d and nd fits before the change therefore estimated a slightly different model from the one that generated the trees, a candidate explanation for \(\beta_D\) returning at 0.43-0.82 of its value in every D experiment (E19, E30, E31, E37); E44 measured the difference on complete trees. The same commit changed lineage_table()'s Poisson rows, which covariate_path() reads: they took \(D\) at the segment's midpoint and now take its constant value on the segment, so every path result with a \(D\) column (E20, E21b, E22, E33, and the observed-tree paths of E26, E28 and E40) was computed on the earlier rows.
  • Unfinished fits and binding boxes. Most lineage-level fits ended on their iteration cap or clock, and on real trees many parameters end on a box edge (E40, E41).

Phase 0 — make the measurement possible

0.1 One definition of D (decision). Done: emphasis 4e0b9dd makes the likelihood hold \(D\) constant on a segment, as the simulator and the definition do; the model itself does not change. Verified by step 0.2 (E44).

0.2 The complete-data likelihood is the simulator's (E44). For each of the ten models, simulate complete trees (extinct lineages kept, \(\rho = 1\), 100 tips, 40 trees, effects \(\pm 0.35\) per scale unit) and maximise the complete-data likelihood directly — no augmentation, no Monte Carlo. Rule: the median ratio of every coefficient to its generating value is in [0.9, 1.1] and the right sign holds on ≥ 95% of trees. Any model that fails has a likelihood-simulator mismatch, and is fixed before it enters Phase 2. Cost: minutes.

0.3 The conditioning factor (E43). \(P(S \mid \theta, T, \rho)\) — the probability that both crown lineages leave a sampled descendant — does not depend on the observed tree, so one function per model, crown age and sampling fraction serves every fit.

  • cr: the closed form \(p(0)^2\); dd: the master equation on the two crown subclade sizes (sim/E36c-survival.cpp). Passed to estimate_rates(cond = ) as functions: no emulator error.
  • Lineage-level models: an emulator trained once per model on clades from the whole box, with capped clades counted as survived, at 2000, 5000 and 20000 training clades.
  • Measured: (a) accuracy against a direct Monte Carlo estimate with 20000 clades at 200 points drawn near the estimates of the planned cells (unbiased, standard error known); (b) retraining variability — 20 trainings with different seeds, the spread of \(\log \hat P\) and of its gradient at the same points; (c) for cr/dd, the emulator against the exact value at the same points.
  • Rule: an emulator is adequate if, at the points, the 90th percentile of \(|\log \hat P - \log P|\) is at most 0.05 and the retraining standard deviation of \(\log \hat P\) is at most 0.02 — small against the conditioning term itself, which is -0.01 to -0.2 at the estimates seen so far (E34r). Otherwise the training size is raised; if 20000 clades do not suffice, the conditioned fit uses the Monte Carlo estimate at each M-step candidate instead (slower, unbiased).

0.4 Simulations conditioned on survival only. Every generator keeps a tree only if both crown lineages survive to \(T = 15\); no size window. The target size is reached by calibrating the generating parameters (the median tree size of the cell), and the realised sizes are reported and used as the covariate of every curve. The likelihood conditioned on survival is then the correct one for these trees.

0.5 Fits that finish, in boxes that do not bind. From E41: every fit runs under the mc_error rule with up to 1000 iterations, a clock at least four times the measured need, started from the nested model's estimate; a fit that ends on a box edge is refitted with that edge moved out, and a parameter that still follows the edge is reported as "not bounded by the tree" rather than read. A fit that does not finish is reported as unfinished and not read.

Phase 1 — cr and dd against the exact conditioned MLE (E45)

The two models with an exact likelihood, so both questions can be answered: does MCEM reach the exact estimate, and how far is the exact estimate from the truth at each size.

models cr; dd on the exponential link (\(\lambda\), \(\mu\) log-linear in \(N\))
tips 25, 50, 100, 200, 400, 800 (dd to 400)
turnover at the crown 0.10, 0.25, 0.40
\(\rho\) 1, and 0.8 at 100 and 200 tips
trees per cell 40
fits conditioned MCEM (exact survival function); exact conditioned MLE (bd_ML cond = 1; the replaced-rate DDD likelihood of E42 with cond = 1)
read per parameter: median ratio to the generating value, central 80% of the ratio, RMSE; the deficit \(\ell(\hat\theta_{MLE}) - \ell(\hat\theta_{MCEM})\); profile-likelihood 95% interval and its coverage

Rule. MCEM agreement: MCEM reaches the exact maximum-likelihood estimate (median deficit ≥ -0.5 nats, 90th percentile of \(|\Delta\ell| \le 2\)). A parameter is reliable at a size when, in that cell, the median ratio is in [0.8, 1.25], the central 80% of ratios lies within [0.5, 2], and the 95% interval covers the generating value on ≥ 90% of trees. The answer is, per model, parameter and turnover, the smallest size from which the parameter is reliable.

Cost: cr fits are seconds; dd exact maximisation minutes to an hour at 400 tips. About a day.

Folded in. The ESS at the generating values before each fit, which replaces E7's ed and ned cells (as E6b does for E6); the D-proposal question under the corrected likelihood: the 100-tip d and nd cells are fitted on both proposals and E19b's adoption rule is applied; the ned and nk ratios of E27. E46 runs effects of ±0.35 only and turnover 0.10 and 0.40, so E16's turnover 0.25 and the selection contour go to E47.

Conditioning. By E43, the conditioned likelihood of each of the eight models needs a Monte Carlo estimate of \(P(S)\) at every parameter value the M-step evaluates. At a standard error of 0.01 in \(\log P\), one estimate took a median of 2 to 29 seconds and a 90th percentile of 3.6 to 43 minutes of one core in E43's boxes, and each carries noise of about 0.01 in \(\log P\), while the M-step evaluates \(\log P\) hundreds to thousands of times an iteration. E46 therefore estimates \(\log P(S)\) at a few dozen points around the unconditioned estimate and fits a quadratic surface to them, rebuilding the surface whenever the fit moves out of its range; the surfaces cost 0.4-9% of a fit's time in the pilot.

Rule. As Phase 1 without the coverage condition (an MCEM fit returns no interval), with two additions: a coefficient is identified only if its sign is right on ≥ 95% of trees; a cell is read only if ≥ 80% of its fits finished and its median effective sample is ≥ 10 (otherwise it is reported as clock-limited, unfinished or sampler-limited, which is itself the answer for that size). The registered rules, including the noise of the conditioning itself, are on E46.

Cost: about 10 500 CPU-hours for the 50- and 100-tip cells (about 14 days on 24 workers), each 200-tip cell 3.1 times its 100-tip cell; the sizes run upwards so the curves fill in from the small end.

Phase 3 — model choice (E47)

Once Phase 2 says which parameters are reliable at which size: the conditioned ladder on the same trees and on null trees, read by the parsimony rule. It needs an effect grid for the selection contour, since E46 runs only ±0.35 (E12, E16, E18). Folded in: the path suite under the current lineage_table(), both routes on E46's trees (E20, E21b, E22, E33 (b) at the generating parameters); an alternative-generator bootstrap for K (E17's open question); E27's \(\rho\) 0.75 and 0.50 cells and family-like cell; and E35 (b)'s misstated \(\rho\). Order and dependencies

0.1 (decision) → 0.2 (minutes) → 0.3 (a day) and 0.4 (calibration, hours) → Phase 1 (a day) → Phase 2 (about 14 days for 50 and 100 tips, then larger sizes where needed). Phase 0 ran (E44, E43) while E40 finished; Phase 1 (E45) runs on the workers E37 released on 2026-10-15.
