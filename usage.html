Usage: one session, from a simulated tree to a chosen model

This page runs the package once, on one tree, through every route the overview describes: a tree is simulated under a diversity-dependent model, the ladder of nested models is fitted to its reconstructed tree, the fits are compared, the covariate path is traced on the observed tree, the constant-rate fit is conditioned on survival through its exact probability and through the survival emulator, the Monte Carlo EM run is inspected, and the earlier four-stage pipeline is run on the same tree. Every chunk was executed in the order shown, in one R session, and the block under each chunk is its console output as the session wrote it. A sentence after each block says what to read in it.

The budgets are small so that the whole session runs in minutes: 100 augmented trees per E-step and 25 EM iterations where the package defaults are 200 and 200. The estimates therefore stop at the iteration cap rather than at the stopping rule, and their Monte Carlo digits are those of a 100-draw estimate. The fits are not the measurements the experiments report, which use the defaults or more; they show the calls, the objects they return and how to read them.

1. The session

The session ran on 19 October 2026 under R 4.3.3 on x86_64 Linux with emphasis 0.4 at commit 4e0b9dd, the commit that holds \(D\) constant between events (the later commits on the branch change the README and a test, not the package), with ape 5.8.1 and mgcv 1.9.1. The package is installed with

remotes::install_github("franciscorichter/emphasis")

Two things about reproducibility. set.seed() fixes the forward simulator, so the tree of Section 2 is the same on every machine. The fits use num_threads = 4, and augmentation on more than one thread is not reproducible: each worker draws from its own substream and the sample keeps the first draws to finish, so a re-run gives the same tree and fits that differ in their Monte Carlo digits. The elapsed and CPU time of each chunk are in Section 9.

2. A simulated tree

simulate_tree() runs the forward process from the crown. The generator is the diversity-dependent model dd on the exponential link, \(\lambda(N) = 0.5\,e^{-0.02 N}\) and \(\mu = 0.1\), over a crown age of 10. The parameter vector follows the layout of the overview's model table: the \(\beta\) coefficients of the active covariates, then the \(\gamma\) coefficients.

library(emphasis)
library(ape)

set.seed(6)
theta <- c(beta_0 = log(0.5), beta_N = -0.02, gamma_0 = log(0.1), gamma_N = 0)
sim <- simulate_tree(pars = theta, max_t = 10, model = "dd", link = "exponential")
sim$status
phy <- sim$tes
c(extant_tips = Ntip(phy), tips_with_extinct = Ntip(sim$tas), crown_age = max(branching.times(phy)))
[1] "done"
      extant_tips tips_with_extinct         crown_age 
               59                83                10

status is "done": the clade reached the present at the first attempt. The reconstructed tree tes has 59 tips and the complete tree tas 83, so 24 lineages arose and went extinct before the present and are what the fits below have to integrate over. At 59 lineages the generating speciation rate is \(0.5\,e^{-1.18} = 0.15\), against an extinction rate of 0.10. phy is the only object the rest of the session reads; theta is kept to compare the estimates with.

5. The covariate path

covariate_path() traces one differential-geometric LARS path over the covariates a rate can read, on the lineage table of the tree, and picks a point on it by BIC. Given a single phylo it treats the tree as fully observed: the speciation path is exact, and the extinction path has no event to read.

The covariates enter the speciation path in the order \(K\), \(\mathrm{ED}\), \(D\), \(N\), at \(\gamma\) = 1.04, 0.58, 0.36 and 0.05, and BIC keeps \(K\) alone, with a coefficient of \(-0.168\) per ancestral split on the log rate and an intercept of \(-0.749\), a rate of \(e^{-0.749} = 0.47\) at \(K = 0\). \(N\), the generating covariate, enters last. The lineage table has 1,769 rows, one per lineage and segment, and 57 events, the \(n - 2\) splits a 59-tip tree records after the crown. The extinction path has 0 events, since no extinction is observed on a reconstructed tree. On a single observed tree the path reads the tree as complete, with 24 lineages missing from it, which is why the overview calls it a diagnostic of which covariates the data ask for first and not a route of record (Section 9); on the augmented trees of an E-step, with their weights, the same call gives the path the M-step sees.

6. Conditioning on survival

The likelihood is conditioned on the survival of both crown lineages when estimate_rates() is given a survival probability through cond (overview, Section 8). Under cr that probability is exact: with \(d = \lambda - \mu\) and crown age \(T\), one lineage survives with probability \(p = d/(\lambda - \mu e^{-dT})\) and the clade with probability \(p^2\). A function of the parameter data frame is the first of the three forms cond accepts, and predict_survival() evaluates any of the three.

age <- max(branching.times(phy))
p_cr <- function(pars) {
  lambda <- exp(pars$beta_0); mu <- exp(pars$gamma_0); d <- lambda - mu
  p <- ifelse(abs(d) < 1e-12, 1 / (1 + lambda * age), d / (lambda - mu * exp(-d * age)))
  p^2
}
theta_cr <- as.data.frame(t(fits$fits$cr$pars))
theta_cr
predict_survival(p_cr, theta_cr)
     beta_0   gamma_0
1 -1.414223 -4.313994
[1] 0.9029204

At the unconditioned cr estimate, \(\lambda = e^{-1.414} = 0.243\) and \(\mu = e^{-4.314} = 0.013\), one crown lineage survives 10 time units with probability 0.950 and the clade with probability \(0.950^2 = 0.903\). The function takes a data frame with the parameter names of the fit, which is the form cond passes it.

The second form is the binomial GAM that auto_bounds() trains over its box, 2,000 simulated clades labelled survived or extinct. The grid compares it with the exact probability at four parameter values.

set.seed(2)
ab_cr <- auto_bounds(phy, model = "cr", link = "exponential", verbose = FALSE)
grid <- data.frame(beta_0 = log(c(0.3, 0.3, 0.5, 0.5)), gamma_0 = log(c(0.05, 0.2, 0.05, 0.2)))
cbind(grid, exact = predict_survival(p_cr, grid), gam = predict_survival(ab_cr$survival_gam, grid))

Conditioning moves the speciation rate from 0.243 to 0.237 and the extinction rate from 0.0134 to 0.0040, a factor of 3.4: the extinction side is the parameter the survival term acts on. 8. The pipeline

emphasis_pipeline() is the earlier workflow kept for compatibility (overview, Section 11): auto_bounds() with its survival GAM, a likelihood surface fitted on a grid and maximised, a cross-entropy search over the box, and MCEM from the CEM result, conditioned on the GAM. The grid and the search are cut to a fraction of their defaults here (30 grid points of 30 draws where the defaults are 150 of 200; 16 particles over 3 iterations where the defaults are 50 over 20).

set.seed(3)
pl <- emphasis_pipeline(phy, model = "dd", link = "exponential",
                        control = list(num_threads = 4,
                                       gam = list(n_grid = 30, sample_size = 30),
                                       cem = list(num_particles = 16, max_iter = 3),
                                       mcem = list(sample_size = 100, max_iter = 25)),
                        verbose = FALSE)
pl
rbind(generating = exp(theta), pipeline = exp(pl$pars), ladder_dd = exp(fits$fits$dd$pars))

The stage log gives each stage's time: 34.0 s for the bounds and the survival GAM, 2.4 s for the surface on the 24 of the 30 grid points at which the likelihood estimate was finite, 4.8 s for the cross-entropy search and 11.3 s for the 25 MCEM iterations. Its log-likelihood \(-135.53\) is conditioned on the survival GAM and is not compared with the ladder's unconditioned \(-133.92\). The last table is on the rate scale: the intercepts are rates and the slopes are factors per lineage. The pipeline's speciation intercept is 0.549 against a generating 0.5 and the ladder's 0.710; the two \(N\) factors, 0.978 and 0.979, sit just below the generating 0.980; the extinction intercept is 0.0040 against a generating 0.1, and the extinction slope sits at the box edge in both fits. Extinction is the side a reconstructed tree carries least, and at this size and turnover the measurements say it is not read (overview, Section 10).

9. What the session cost

Elapsed and CPU seconds of each chunk on four threads of an AMD Ryzen 9 9955HX, as proc.time() measured them around each chunk.

The session took 538 s elapsed and 862 s of CPU, 83% of the elapsed time in the ladder, whose five rungs each ran 25 E-steps of 100 draws and a last E-step at the estimate. The ratio of CPU to elapsed time is 1.5 on the ladder and 2.9 on the pipeline against the 4 threads asked for: the parts of the run that are R code use one thread: the surrogate proposal's draws and their mean-field sweep (R/bdi.R), and the auto_bounds() calls of the ladder, which does not pass num_threads on to them; the thinning proposal, the likelihood and the M-step use four, as do the forward simulations of auto_bounds() inside the pipeline. At the package defaults, 200 draws and up to 200 iterations, the same ladder costs up to 16 times the E-step work shown here; the ladder on 170 real trees of 50 to 200 tips ran with 300 draws and 300 iterations under a four-hour clock per fit (Real phylogenies).
