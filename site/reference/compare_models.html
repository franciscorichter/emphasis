Description

Ranks two or more fits from estimate_rates by AIC. Each fit's log-likelihood is a log of a Monte Carlo mean, so it sits below the log-likelihood it estimates; how far below grows as that fit's own effective sample falls, and the amounts do not cancel between models. A model that is harder to sample is therefore penalised for being harder to sample, and the AIC difference between two fits can be smaller than the difference in their biases.

Usage

compare_models(..., margin = 2)

Arguments

...

Two or more emphasis_fit objects. Names become the row labels; unnamed arguments are labelled from the fitted model.

margin

AIC units within which two models are not told apart. Default 2.

Details

The returned table carries ESS and loglik_gap per fit and AIC_swing, twice the gap relative to the leader's, which is how much of the AIC difference the bias alone could account for.

ranking_at_risk names the models that could overtake the leader once both biases are corrected. A model qualifies only if its own gap is larger than the leader's — so that correcting both closes the distance — and then either the swing reaches its delta_AIC or its draw is heavy enough that the swing understates the gap. A warning is issued with it.

Two things follow that a caller should not have to infer. The attribute is about the ORDER, not about precision: when the leader is itself the heavily-handicapped fit, correcting the biases only widens its lead, so nothing is named and nothing is wrong with that. And it covers bias, not variance. A fit can lead on a log-likelihood estimated from a handful of effective draws — measured: a winner with an effective sample of 6.5 of 300 — and be named by nothing here, because its expected position is safe while its realised one is noisy. loglik_se and ESS are the columns for that, and they are in the table to be read.

The row with the lowest AIC is not the model the table chooses. Among nested models the richer one has the lower AIC whenever its two extra parameters raise the log-likelihood by more than 2, which they do on a share of trees generated without them: with two extra parameters the likelihood-ratio statistic is chi-squared on two degrees of freedom under the null, so the richer model takes the minimum on about 13% of null trees, and with three richer models offered beside the null, on more. Measured, on 20 trees a cell from a diversity-dependent generator at 50 to 200 tips: a lineage-level model had the minimum AIC on 2 to 7 of 20, and led by more than 2 AIC on 0 to 2 of 20 (E27). The chosen attribute is therefore the model with the fewest parameters among those within margin AIC units of the minimum, and within_margin marks those rows; the full ranking is kept so that the margin can be read.

A warning names the chosen model when its own effective sample is below 10 draws: its log-likelihood is then an estimate from a handful of histories and the margin it rests on is noise as much as evidence. The ranking_at_risk attribute covers the bias of unequal precision; this covers its variance.

Value

A data frame, one row per fit, with columns model, n_pars, loglik, AIC, loglik_se, ESS, loglik_gap, gap_heavy, delta_AIC, AICw, AIC_swing and within_margin, ordered by AIC, with attributes chosen (the most parsimonious model within margin of the minimum), chosen_starved (whether that model's effective sample is below 10) and ranking_at_risk.

Examples

set.seed(1)
tr  <- simulate_tree(pars = c(0.5, 0.1), max_t = 4, model = "cr")
phy <- tr$tes
ctrl <- list(max_iter = 5, num_trees = 30)
fit_cr <- estimate_rates(phy, method = "mcem", model = "cr",
  control = c(ctrl, list(lower_bound = c(0, 0), upper_bound = c(2, 1))))
fit_dd <- estimate_rates(phy, method = "mcem", model = "dd",
  control = c(ctrl, list(lower_bound = c(0, -0.1, 0, -0.1),
                         upper_bound = c(2, 0.1, 1, 0.1))))
compare_models(cr = fit_cr, dd = fit_dd)
