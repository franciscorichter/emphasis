Description

\(\widehat\ell = \log(\frac1N \sum_i w_i)\) estimates \(\log E[w]\), and a log of a mean is not the mean of a log: by Jensen's inequality \(E[\widehat\ell] \le \log E[w]\), always downward, never up. The EM objective is unaffected – it is an expectation of a log, and the M-step averages \(\log f\) directly (src/M_step.cpp) – but the log-likelihood a fit reports is exactly this estimator, and so is the AIC built from it.

Usage

.jensen_gap(ess, n)

Arguments

ess

Effective sample size of the draw.

n

Number of draws the estimator averaged over.

Details

The delta method gives the bias as \(-\mathrm{Var}(w)/(2N E[w]^2)\), which in terms of the effective sample size \(n_{\mathrm{eff}} = N/(1+\mathrm{CV}^2)\) is \(-\frac12(1/n_{\mathrm{eff}} - 1/N)\): a quantity already on record for every fit.

It is an indicator of size, not a bound in either direction, and its error runs the wrong way where the quantity matters. Measured on log-normal weights with \(N = 200\), as the ratio of what this returns to the true bias: 4.9 at \(n_{\mathrm{eff}} = 188\), 2.0 at 157, 0.90 at 82, 0.75 at 36, 0.46 at 17, 0.27 at 9, 0.16 at 6. It overstates only where the bias is negligible anyway (below \(2\times10^{-4}\)), and understates once the weights are heavy – by a factor of six at \(n_{\mathrm{eff}} = 6\), where the true bias is half a nat. heavy marks that regime, \(n_{\mathrm{eff}} < N/10\), which is where the ratio first falls below one half. A caller deciding anything on the size of the gap must treat a heavy draw as unquantified rather than as the number returned.

Value

A list: gap (a non-negative magnitude, the amount by which the reported log-likelihood sits below the truth) and heavy (TRUE when gap understates it).
