Complete trees: does the likelihood return the true parameters?

We simulated 40 complete trees (every lineage, the extinct ones included) in each of 64 settings of the ten models, and fitted each tree by its own model, maximising the likelihood directly. There is no Monte Carlo here and nothing hidden: this checks the likelihood itself.

The answer

  • Intercepts come back. The speciation intercept is within 10% of its true value in 62 of 64 settings, and the extinction intercept within [0.8, 1.25] in 61 of 64: a complete tree records its extinctions. The exceptions are k at +0.35 (0.59 to 0.76).
  • Single covariate slopes come back. The slopes of D, K and centred or single ED are within 10% in 43 of 50 settings (range 0.86 to 1.20).
  • The diversity slope is too steep. βN is within 10% in 17 of 30 settings; elsewhere it is 1.10 to 1.39 times its value, mostly at turnover 0.40.
  • Distinctiveness beside N is unstable. In ned its slope ranges from 0.68 to 1.68 times its value.
  • Pooled over each setting’s 40 trees (one estimate for all of them), 138 of 150 speciation coefficients are within 10% (intercepts on the rate scale, as the rule reads them; 135 on the log scale, as the methods paper reads them). Much of the per-tree scatter is therefore the small size of one tree. Of the 12 left, 7 are βN, off by 14–21%: too steep in five settings at turnover 0.40, too shallow in two.

The registered rule, which asks every coefficient of a setting to be within 10% and its bootstrap interval to contain the true value and the slope sign to be right on 95% of trees, holds in 29 of 64 settings.

Design

models all ten, exponential link
settings 100 and 200 tips, turnover 0.10 and 0.40; lineage-level effect ±0.35 on speciation, extinction constant; 64 reachable settings
trees 40 a setting, complete, crown age 15, both crown lineages surviving: 2560 trees
fit the complete-data log-likelihood, maximised by Subplex from the true values and a perturbed start
