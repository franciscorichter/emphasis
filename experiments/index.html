These are the pre-registered experiments behind the emphasis package: each tests the Monte Carlo EM estimator against an exact answer or a generating value, or asks what a dated tree can estimate. The validation series V1–V12 validates every method the two papers use, on one build (1.0.0.9000) under one protocol: calibrated generators with no size window, the likelihood conditioned on survival, the exponential link, and fits run until the Monte Carlo error rule stops them. The experiments E1–E48 before it are the earlier record; each row below says what of it holds now. The conclusions that hold now are collected in What we know and the terms in the Glossary.

Identifiable, estimable, informative

These pages keep three properties apart, because each belongs to a different thing.

  • Identifiable is a property of the model together with what is observed: a reconstructed tree of the sampled extant lineages, the sampling fraction \(\rho\), conditioning on survival. Distinct parameter values give distinct distributions of trees. It holds or fails for every tree at once, and no tree size repairs a failure: rates that vary freely in time are not identifiable from a reconstructed tree (Louca and Pennell 2020).
  • Estimable at \(n\) tips is a property of the model and the design (size, turnover, effect size, \(\rho\)): an identifiable parameter is recovered within an experiment's rule on trees of that design. This is what the simulation experiments measure. Extinction under constant rates is identifiable, and at turnover 0.10 it is not estimable up to 1600 tips (E38).
  • The information a tree carries is a property of one tree under a model: how sharply its likelihood falls away from the maximum, read from a profile or from a fit that ends on the edge of the parameter box. A tree with little spread in pendant ages says little about \(\beta_D\) even at a size where \(\beta_D\) is estimable, and one such tree is not evidence that the parameter is not identifiable.

The simulation experiments report the second, the real-tree analyses (E26, E28, E40, E48) the third. Experiment pages say "identified at \(n\) tips" in the second sense.

Reading a page

Every experiment page has the same headings: Question, Design, Pre-set rule (as registered before the data existed), Results, Conclusion, Related. Variants (E8b, E10b, E12b, E19b, E24b, E34r, E36c and others) are sections of the parent page. Three markings recur:

  • sampler-limited: the cell's median effective sample is below 10; its rates are reported, not read against the rule.
  • clock-limited: the wall clock ended more than 10% of the cell's fits; its rates are floors.
  • unfinished: the fits ran to their iteration cap without meeting the stopping rule.
