emphasis fits birth-death diversification models to a dated phylogeny by Monte Carlo EM: the hidden part of the tree (extinct and unsampled lineages) is drawn by importance sampling under one of two proposals, and the expected complete-data log-likelihood is maximised over the draws. Its speciation and extinction rates can depend on the number of lineages \(N\) and on three lineage-level covariates: the centred pendant age \(D\), evolutionary distinctiveness ED (raw or centred) and the count of ancestral splits \(K\). That gives ten named models (cr, dd, d, nd, ed, edc, ned, nedc, k, nk; see the Glossary). The experiments test whether the estimator returns the exact maximum-likelihood estimate where one exists (cr, dd), with which proposal and at what cost; whether lineage-level coefficients are recovered and selected against their generating values; how a model is chosen (the one-call ladder and the dgLARS covariate path); what a reconstructed tree says about extinction; and what the workflow returns on real family and class-tree clades. The conclusions across all of them are in What we know; the record-versus-data conflicts and pending decisions in Open issues.

How to read a page

Every experiment page has the same headings, in this order:

heading what it holds
(top table) status, package build, driver and analysis scripts, data files
Question why the experiment was run, and which earlier result led to it
Design what was simulated or fitted, with a parameter table
Pre-set rule the decision rule as registered in 00-design.md before the data existed
Results the numbers, a table, and at least one figure with a caption saying what to read off it
Conclusion the verdict under the rule (met / not met / not evaluable), what it means, what it does not show
Related the experiments that led to it and followed from it

Variants of an experiment (E8b, E10b, E12b, E14b, E19b, E21a/b, E24b, E31b, E34r, E36c, …) are sections inside the parent page. Three markings recur in tables and verdicts:

  • sampler-limited: the cell's median effective sample (ESS) is below 10; its rates are reported and not read against the rule.
  • clock-limited: the wall clock ended more than 10% of the fits in the cell (the convention fixed on 2026-10-07 after E31); its rates are floors.
  • unfinished: the fits ran to their iteration cap without meeting the stopping rule.

Chronology

The study opened with the exact comparisons E1 and S and the first ned measurement E2, then turned to the proposals (E3, E4, E6); S's failed ned arm led to the feasibility map E7, and its clocked cr fits to the stopping-rule check E9, the relaxation sweep E10 and the noise study E13. E8 found ED estimable but not selected at one point, which E12 turned into a power map; E5, the first run on real trees, placed three bird families at turnover up to 0.3, so E16 repeated the map at 0.25 and E17 bootstrapped the families' margins; E18 tried centring ED, and E11, gated by E14, tested specificity on another group's simulator. The pendant-age model (E19) and the ancestral-splits covariate (E21) followed, with the covariate path (E20, E22) as a second route to model choice and E15's profile showing that the time of a fit is in its starting point, which gave the ladder its nested starts. The one-call ladder (E23) and the single-covariate models (E24) preceded the real-data runs E25 and E26, which E27 calibrated and E28 refitted as the analysis of record. Longer budgets at 200 tips (E29, E31) exposed the extinction side on the box limits, which E32, E34, E36, E35 and E38 traced to the trees' lack of information rather than the box, the conditioning or the emulator; E30 and E37 ask whether the pendant-age fit ever finishes. E33 tested the path as a user runs it, E39 measured how K and ED overlap on real trees, and E40, E41 and E42 carried the workflow to clades cut from the bird and mammal class trees (E40 closed on 2026-10-18, E41 at its pilot on 2026-10-19). The estimability study under the conditioned likelihood opened with E44 and E43, and E45 is running.
